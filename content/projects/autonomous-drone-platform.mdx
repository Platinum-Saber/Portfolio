## The problem

Autonomous flight in cluttered, GPS-denied spaces only works when the whole stack does. It
needs a depth sensor that returns usable geometry indoors, and enough onboard compute to
close the perception loop without a ground station. It also needs an airframe stiff enough
that vibration doesn't destroy the state estimate.

This is my final year project - building that platform end to end rather than buying it.

## Approach

I took the Stanford MSL **TrajBridge** drone as the hardware reference design and worked
outward from the compute and sensing I already had.

**Perception and compute.** An **Orbbec Gemini 336** depth camera paired with an
**NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano** for onboard inference. The Orin Nano is the pivot of the design. It can run depth processing and a navigation
policy onboard, so the aircraft doesn't depend on a radio link to stay alive.

**Airframe.** I originally planned a 3D-printed frame and abandoned it after working the
numbers: for the stiffness required it came out both **too heavy and too weak** compared to
carbon fibre. The design moved to **laser-cut carbon fibre sheet** for the primary
structure, keeping 3D printing for non-structural parts - the **Nylon 12 prop ducts**,
mounts and sensor brackets - where its geometric freedom actually pays for itself.

**Propulsion.** Four **2205 motors** on 5 in three-blade props inside the ducts, on a
222 mm diagonal, sized against the mass budget the carbon frame and the Orin Nano impose.

## Where it stands

In progress. The full airframe is modelled in CAD, and the compute and sensing selection
is settled; the current work is sourcing propulsion and integration.

## What I took from it

The frame decision was the useful lesson. The 3D-printed version was the obvious path, and the analysis said no. Convenient
manufacturing is not the same as appropriate manufacturing, and on an airframe the mass
budget arbitrates every argument.
